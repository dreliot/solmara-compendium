Solmara was once a powerful nation, but years of apocalyptic wildfires—remembered as the Ashfall Fires or the Ashfall Years—devastated the country and left its central government with little power. Ashfall lies beyond living memory. Stories of the Golden Age before the fires survive alongside ruined buildings, but there are several contested accounts of what caused the disaster and what happened during it. Many of these accounts blame some form of greed or overreach, while a widespread belief holds that the outside world abandoned Solmara when it needed help most.

Today, Solmara is a loose federation of municipalities and local governments occupying the former nation’s increasingly porous territory. A Senate remains in the capital, where it passes laws, makes major decisions, and arbitrates disputes between local governments and organizations such as the Grove Cooperative. Its laws, however, are enforced very unevenly. Some communities identify strongly with their Solmaraan heritage, while others may symbolically reject it. Shared religious, cultural, and ecological traditions nevertheless continue to connect much of the region.

Conditions vary greatly from one community to another. Most settlements are small and interdependent, and their security depends heavily on their access to magic users, technology, infrastructure, and resources. Ashfall caused a major technological regression by destroying much of the infrastructure and material supply needed to sustain the old world’s development. Travel is generally limited to horseback and established dirt roads, but communities remain connected through private couriers, markets, festivals, arcane radio, traveling rig-menders, and the Greenkeepers.

The need to survive Ashfall forced many communities to adopt more interdependent ways of living. From that experience comes a precarious hope: that Solmara remains a place of possibility and rebirth, and that its people might build something better than their forebears and avert another disaster. This hope is neither universal nor consistent. Even among those who share it, there is no single vision of what a better Solmara should become.
