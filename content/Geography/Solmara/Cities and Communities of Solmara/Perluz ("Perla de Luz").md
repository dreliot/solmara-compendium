### The political equilibrium

The current balance is roughly a **five-way standoff held in place by mutual dependence**:
1. The **Zanjeros** hold the gates but not the guns.
2. The **Houses** have the oldest water rights and the most social prestige.
3. The **Barons** have fuel, money, and the most guns.
4. The **Cabildo and the Sisters** have moral legitimacy and nothing else.
5. The **Wellwrights and the Flats** have numbers and craft, and they’re the ones who suffer when the equilibrium shifts.

Nobody can seize the Catarra without the others uniting against them, and nobody wants an open war because the water system would break and everyone would lose. The result is a cold, ritualized politics full of arbitration, quiet bribes, and exquisitely polite threats.

### The Flats are not just victims

I’d give them real texture so they’re more than a downtrodden background:
- **Neighborhood blocs** that organize around a shared well or cistern, each with its own customs and a block captain.
- **Bell-parish solidarity** through the Sisters, which crosses factional lines.
- **A thriving informal economy**: repair, salvage, brewing, tar-fuel cooking, and the resale of reclaimed materials from the Dead Zones.
- **A culture of mutual aid** in the cozy-apocalyptic spirit, which makes the Flats the place where the setting’s warmth lives, even in the middle of this much hardship.

### How Perluz relates to the rest of Solmara

- **The Rangers** avoid it, and every faction reads that avoidance differently. The Houses assume it’s contempt, the Barons assume it’s fear, and the Sisters suspect something older. The Rangers’ real reason can stay hidden.
- **Ossadavia** has historically sent scholars to the Hoya, and may still want access to it, so it has an interest in whoever controls the shrine.
- **Sana Amaris** is the Breakwater Houses’ trade partner and a possible source of mediation or disruption.
- **Mistgate** probably regards Perluz with wary distance and cultural snobbery.

### Where the tension snaps

A few pressure points that can drive a campaign:
- **A rumor that the Zanjeros plan to hand the Catarra to one patron.** Everyone scrambles.
- **A drought year or a failed reservoir**, which exposes how thin the equilibrium is.
- **The solar still works.** If the Breakwater Houses reveal or prove a new water source, the whole leverage structure changes overnight.
- **The Drained Valley acts**, either with a demand or a sabotage.
- **A new party tries to reopen the Hoya**, whether a Baron wanting deeper drilling or a pilgrim movement trying to stop them.

### The Brea Pits as Umbora’s shrine

The early-1900s detail helps here. The pits were being mined for asphalt, and scholars were just beginning to dig out the bones. In Solmara, Ossadavia scholars may have excavated here before the Rangers’ avoidance policy made them leave, which fits Ossadavia’s own name. The shrine can be small and made of tar-black stone, with condor feathers left as offerings. Pilgrims can bury tokens in the seeps, and the bones that come back up are treated as messages. One hook is that the Derricks’ underground fires sometimes burn near the shrine. Some seekers insist the tar fire is the Forever Burn from the Mother’s myth, which makes LA a convincing false lead for anyone hunting that hidden site.

### The Drained Valley

[The Drained Valley](geo:36.6156297,-118.1095779)
The aqueduct didn’t carry Perluz’s own water. It carried a distant mountain valley’s water, taken by deals made quietly before the valley’s people understood what was happening. In Solmara, call it **the Drained Valley**, a placeholder analog for the Owens Valley, far to the north and east. Its people never forgave Perluz, and since Ashfall they’ve had no reason to share anything.
