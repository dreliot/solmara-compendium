## Pitch

Several generations after civilization collapsed during years of apocalyptic wildfires known as [[The Ashfall Years]], small communities across fire-scarred, magic-touched Solmara are rebuilding — not back to what was, but into something new, stitched together from salvaged technology, renewed tradition, and an evolving relationship with the land itself.

## Setting Anchor

**[[Solmara]]** — a fantasy analogue of California. Geography, climate zones, and ecosystems drawn from the real state, but renamed throughout. Naming approach favors sound and phonetic texture (soft open vowels, rolling consonants, Spanish/Mexican-influenced cadence) over direct translation — evoking cultural resonance without translating or repurposing real words. Cultural texture draws from Californio/Mexican heritage, cowboy/rancher tradition, and mission-era Catholic folklore, remixed rather than reproduced 1:1.

## Tone

**Cozy-apocalyptic.** Real danger exists — fire season, scarcity, isolated communities, things and places that didn't survive the collapse — but the default emotional register is warmth, competence, and found-family. Closer to _Station Eleven_'s quiet dignity and Becky Chambers' character-first optimism than grimdark survival horror. Le Guin-style anthropological curiosity about how the surviving cultures actually _work_.

## Literary/Tonal Touchstones

- **Becky Chambers** — warmth, competence-as-kindness, low-stakes-feeling scenes with real emotional weight
- **Ursula K. Le Guin** — ecological and cultural worldbuilding, magic tied to knowledge and relationship rather than domination
- **Emily St. John Mandel (_Station Eleven_)** — collapse as a quiet, generations-past backdrop rather than an active crisis; survivors who are building, not just surviving
- **Solarpunk** — renewable energy/technology and magic coexisting; optimism about rebuilding grounded in ecological realism, not denial of hardship

## Magic

Uncommon — a specialist's tool rather than a common household ability. Practiced by trained institutions rather than innate to the population at large. Tied closely to ecological literacy: growth, fire behavior, water, land health. **[Ranger Order]** is the primary institution embodying this — magic as vocation and public service, not personal power.

## Key Institution: [Ranger Order] _(name pending)_

A pseudo-Park-Ranger/arborist/druidic organization. Rangers:

- Survey forests before and after fire events
- Replant and tend regrowth
- Provide environmental guidance to farming communities
- Walk and maintain specific assigned trails/routes on a recurring basis — part mail carrier, part environmental steward
- Function as the primary link between isolated communities and accumulated post-collapse knowledge

## Pantheon (seed)

Matriarchal pantheon, all deities female or non-binary. Origin figure is **[Mother Deity]** _(name pending)_ — a virgin-mother figure, echoing a folklore-drifted, generations-removed version of Catholic Marian imagery ("thousands of years of telephone" from an original devotional figure).

**Origin myth — the Mother's becoming, and Cazira's birth:** In the young world, before the Mother was a goddess, she was simply a woman — provided for by a bountiful, untouched earth, friend to every bird and beast and fish, wanting for nothing material. She took no more than she needed and gave no less than was asked. And yet she was sad: every creature had language to sing to another, and though she could mimic their songs, she could never understand them. She longed for someone to sing _to._

One day, overcome with grief, she sat beneath a tiny redwood sapling and wept — and her tears watered it until it grew tall enough to touch the heavens. The tree carried her sadness to the stars, and the stars answered with a storm, weeping alongside her. A bolt of lightning split the redwood in two; it began to burn continuously, and at the center of the burn was a heart that the fire would not consume. Pulled toward it, the woman ate the burning heart — and in that moment felt a stirring in her womb. Months later, **[[Cazira]]** — Fire Goddess, firstborn of the Mother's children — was born.

This is the moment the woman becomes the Mother: divinity arrives not through combat or creation from nothing, but through grief, longing, and a strange communion with fire. She conceives without the help of men; all her children are born in unusual, non-standard ways. Eventually she tires of her children's conflicts and **leaves them in charge** — a divine abdication that mirrors the human situation: the old caretakers are gone, and those left behind are now the adults.

_(Confirmed: the tree is a redwood — the myth doubles as a just-so story for why redwoods are the tallest trees in the world, grown by grief-tears reaching for the heavens. Confirmed: the site still exists, and still burns. A small, obscure temple is devoted to it — but its existence is contested. Many treat it as pure legend; only a few believe it's real, and fewer still know where it actually is. It is deliberately **not** a major pilgrimage site on the scale of a real-world Mecca — no mass following, no established pilgrimage route, no fixed place on common maps. It's the kind of place a specific devotee, a Ranger, or a PC might spend a whole personal arc trying to confirm or find, rather than something the setting treats as common knowledge.)_

_(Banked motif: manzanita's real-world trait of resprouting from burls after fire is strong thematic material — worth using elsewhere as a symbol of post-fire resilience/regrowth, e.g. tied to the Ranger Order or general folk symbolism, even though it's not the origin-myth tree.)_

**Known daughters:**
- **[[Cazira]]** — Fire and Sun Goddess, firstborn of the Mother's children. See origin myth above.
- **[[Amaris]]** — goddess of water's movement and life force (fresh and clear water), heavily associated with mirrors and reflection. Twin to Cazira: as a young child, Cazira looked into a tide pool and saw Amaris as her own reflection looking back; when she reached in, she drew her twin to the surface. This origin binds fire and water together at the cosmological level, not just thematically.
- **[[Trem]]** — Earth Goddess, associated with soil, rock, mining, creation, and geological activity — earthquakes especially. Born when Cazira and Amaris, wrestling in conflict, split the earth open between them; the Mother reached into the crack they tore and brought her daughter out into the world. This same crack is the origin of the Sunstone — where Sunstone was first mined  — tying the pantheon's founding myth directly to the setting's signature magical material.
- **[[Umbora]]** — goddess of shadow, death, decay, and rebirth; an embodiment of the carbon cycle. In the dry chaparral hills, a great condor rose from feeding on the dead and took flight toward the sun — but its shadow did not rise with it. It stayed pooled on the sun-baked earth, deepening and thickening where the bird had fed. Trem took the raw shadow that stayed behind and, under the Mother's watch, shaped it into her sister — daughter of shadow, of what remains once death has finished its work, of everything that returns quietly to the ground when it can no longer fly. The condor is her sacred animal. 
- **[[Savia]]**: goddess of growth, change, and time; associated with hearth, home, and harvest. One day, the four goddesses of fire, water, earth and decay saw that their constant squabbling was saddening their mother. They resolved to work together on a project to make Mother happy. Cazira blazed in the sky to make enough sunlight for warmth, Trem shaped and opened up the earth, shaded the plant and provided fertilizer for the soil. In time, Savia grew. When they presented her to Mother, Mother had never been so happy. 

**Pantheon rule:** All deities besides the Mother are her daughters and are sisters to one another — even in cases like Trem's, where a sibling's actions (the twins' fight) created the circumstances of her birth, it was still the Mother who brought her into the world. No deity is born _from_ another deity; the Mother is the sole point of origin.

## Key Hub: [[Ossadavia]]

A fantasy analogue of UC Davis — and, in-world, believed to be built from the actual surviving campus and infrastructure of the pre-collapse university. The name itself is a generations-drifted corruption of "UC Davis," worn down through oral transmission the same way the Mother Deity's name drifted from its original form.

**Character:** Small population relative to its influence — a research-campus-turned-city rather than a sprawling metropolis. Outsized resources and capability compared to its size.

**Population:** Predominantly researchers, technologists, and arcane scientists.

**Function:**

- Headquarters of the [Ranger Order]
- Technological and magical research hub
- Home to massive greenhouses producing luxury goods (sugar, coffee, chocolate) not native to Solmara's climate — implying real magical/technological cultivation capability, not just favorable farmland
- Supplies surrounding communities with these luxuries, making it an economic hub disproportionate to its population size

## Signature Material: [[Sunstone]]

The setting's signature magical material — both sacred and practical.

**Properties:**

- Stores magical energy
- Can be refined into "solar sand"
- Functions as a magical resource/fuel
- Associated with light, fire, and creation

**Functional analogues:** magical batteries, spell components, renewable magical infrastructure — the practical backbone of the solarpunk-meets-magic tone established above.

**Cultural resonance:** evokes California's gold rush history — a valuable, extractable, land-altering resource with its own mythology, labor history, and boom/bust potential built in.

**Mythic origin — intentionally plural, not singular:** Solmara's traditions do not agree on where Sunstone truly comes from, and this is treated as a feature of the setting's mythology rather than a gap to close. At least two accounts coexist:

- Some hold that Sunstone's essential property — fire that does not consume — originates with **Cazira** herself, dating back to the burning, unconsumed heart at the heart of the Mother's founding myth (see Pantheon section). In this telling, Cazira is fire's fundamental nature, and Sunstone simply carries that nature wherever it appears.
- Others hold that Sunstone properly belongs to **Trem**, born at the very fissure where the Forever Burn still rages — her domains of creation, craft, and mining make her the natural patron of the material as a _usable resource_, regardless of fire's deeper nature.
- Many communities and cults blend the two without much concern for consistency: Cazira as the source of the fire, Trem as the source of the stone. Different regions, temples, or Ranger chapters may emphasize one telling over the other, and NPCs devoted to each goddess can genuinely disagree — this is a live source of minor theological friction/color rather than a resolved fact.

## Working Themes

1. **Memory vs. reinvention** — how much of the old world should be preserved, mourned, or deliberately left behind?
2. **Living _with_ the land rather than dominating it** — magic as ecological literacy, not combat
3. **Cultural remixing** — post-collapse communities blending traditions into something new, rather than any single culture "winning" or staying pure

## Central Tension

Fire is not just weather — it's the recurring, half-understood force that ended the old world and still threatens the new one. Live campaign question: **is fire something to be feared, managed, or fundamentally misunderstood — even by the pantheon?**

## Key Cities (in progress)

### [[Mistgate]] _(San Francisco analog)_

Foggy, insular maritime town combined with a cultural/artistic enclave identity. Close-knit community, wary of outsiders. Fog and coastal atmosphere are central to its character — potential tie-in to moisture-harvesting craft/tradition or an artisan culture specific to this climate.

### [LA Analog] _(name pending — likely fragmented, no single name yet)_

Not a unified city post-collapse — an archipelago of independent, wary communities within the old urban footprint. Pre-existing neighborhood income inequality has been sharply exacerbated: walled enclaves of relative wealth/resources exist near genuinely dangerous ruin-zones, with no single authority governing the whole sprawl. May warrant an overall "the ruins of ___" designation for the region, with individual enclave names developed once specific communities are defined.

### [[Sana Amaris]] _(San Diego analog)_

Borderland frontier/trade town with strong, continuously-held Californio culture. Crossroads/marketplace energy — likely the least discontinuous of the three, having remained a living trade hub rather than fracturing or turning insular. Named for the goddess Amaris (water, movement, mirrors) — fitting for a border town where two sides see themselves reflected in each other.
