## Key Antagonist Faction: The Emberkin

Known to outsiders as the **Scorchers** — a fire cult in direct, live conflict with the Grove Cooperative, tied to the campaign's ongoing arson mystery. They are not simple villains: their core theology is a genuine, defensible critique of institutional power, and their leader's founding revelation is real, not delusion.

**Core belief:** Sunstone is sacred and _not itself_ dangerous or corrupt — but no institution, no matter how well-intentioned, can be trusted to hold something this powerful without eventually corrupting it. This is not Cooperative-specific paranoia; it's treated as a near-universal law of institutions. Their theology holds that Cazira's fire — literalized in the "unconsumed heart" of her own origin myth — was never meant to be contained, rationed, or managed, and that concentrating it (the way the Cooperative does with Sunstone reserves) recreates the exact failure that caused Ashfall in the first place — not because Sunstone is the same substance as whatever powered the old world, but because the _pattern of control_ is identical, proving the danger was never the resource, only humanity's relationship to it.

**Goal:** Not seizure of power, not reform, not simple destruction. Permanent decentralization — scattering, destabilizing, and freeing concentrated Sunstone wherever it's found, so that no institution (Cooperative or otherwise) can ever again concentrate this kind of power. She believes this may cost lives, including at civilizational scale, and accepts that cost as necessary. There is real, unresolved dramatic irony here: her diagnosis of the danger may be entirely correct, and her cure may still be exactly what triggers a second Ashfall.

**Relationship to the arson mystery:** Some of the "strange fires" attributed to the Emberkin are genuinely theirs — deliberate destabilization of Sunstone reserves/infrastructure. Others may be failed Cooperative experiments or negligence that the Cooperative quietly blames on the cult rather than admitting fault, since it's a more convenient enemy than institutional failure. Untangling which fire is whose is meant to be a genuine investigative thread, not a simple "find the villain" plot.

**Leader: Iris Solano**, called **The Ember Undying**, or informally **the Ember**, by her followers. Formerly a Grove Cooperative watchtower keeper, posted (with some Ossadavia-adjacent Sunstone training) to a remote, underfunded waystation near a Sunstone mine — the kind of low-priority posting that doesn't get equipment upgrades or real institutional attention. During a wildfire, the mine's Sunstone ignited catastrophically — not ordinary fire, but the same "unquenchable" quality that defines the setting's central mystery — and the blast reached the watchtower directly. She should have died with everyone else. Instead, in the instant the flame took her, she prayed to Cazira, and walked out the other side of the fire unconsumed — a direct, literal echo of the burning heart at the center of Cazira's own origin myth.

She survived to watch the Cooperative arrive not with rescue, but with containment — securing the site, managing the story, treating the disaster as a problem of optics rather than a tragedy demanding response. No one came to check the watchtower. No one asked what she'd seen. As far as Cooperative records are concerned, she died with everyone else that day.

She retreated to a nearby hilltop and remained there in a semi-lucid, monk-like vigil, watching from a distance as the cover-up unfolded below. In that state, she felt a hand come to rest on her shoulder — Cazira's, she has never doubted since — carrying no words, only unmistakable purpose: _you were not wrong, you were not nothing._ She came down from that hill a different woman, let the world go on believing her dead, and began building what would become the Emberkin.

**Personal loss:** _(open thread — worth deciding whether someone specific she loved died in the mine itself, to sharpen her personal grief alongside the institutional betrayal, or whether her radicalization stays rooted purely in what she witnessed rather than personal loss.)_

**Cult self-identity:** **Emberkin** (their own name for themselves — implies chosen family, kinship through shared revelation) vs. **Scorchers** (the dismissive outsider name most ordinary Solmarans actually use). The gap between the two names mirrors the gap between how they understand themselves and how the world sees them.
